# Harbor town

Notes on a small harbor town as described in a family's account books and a few surviving diaries, from the years when the fishing was good and then from the years when it was not.

The account books are mostly about money. Prices of salt and rope, debts owed and paid, a careful record of who bought on credit at the chandler's shop. When the catch is good the entries are crowded and confident. When it fails, the same names appear again and again under what is owed, and the hand that writes them grows smaller.

The diaries are about marriage as much as anything else. Who is engaged, who has married whom, which wedding the whole town came to and which one was hurried and quiet. A daughter writes that she will not marry a man for his fortune, then three pages later admits that she has been thinking about his house on the hill.

Running through all of it is the sea. Boats go out and come back, or do not come back. A widow keeps the shop open for twenty years after her husband's ship is lost, and the account books in her hand are the neatest of all. Her diary has one line about hope, written the year the harbor wall was finally rebuilt.
