# Sayings

Short sayings collected from the letters, journals and diaries, one to a paragraph.

On waiting: Waiting is not the same as doing nothing. The fisherman mending nets on the shore is waiting for the weather, and his hands are never still.

On letters: A letter is a promise that someone will read it later. We write to the people we expect to see again, and sometimes to the ones we will not.

On grief: Grief does not leave so much as change its shape. In the first year it is a storm; later it is weather, something you dress for and go out in anyway.

On the sea: The sea takes and gives back on its own schedule. Every harbor town keeps two calendars, the one on the wall and the one in the tide tables.

On memory: Memory is a house we keep rebuilding from the same stones. The rooms move, but the view from the window stays the same.

On hope: Hope after loss is quieter than hope before it. It expects less and notices more: the first warm morning, a light left on, a letter in a familiar hand.
