# Letters from the coast

These are notes on a bundle of letters a sailor wrote home over three winters. The paper is thin and the ink has gone brown, but every letter ends the same way: a line about the weather, and a line about coming home.

The first winter he writes about the sea as work. The ship, the nets, the cold that gets into the hands and stays there. He counts the money he will send, down to the shilling, and asks whether the roof was mended before the rains. There is no grief in these letters yet, only distance and a steady wish to be useful to the people waiting for him.

In the second winter a brother is lost overboard in a storm off the point. The letter that tells of it is short. He writes that the water closed over and there was nothing to be done, and then, a page later, that he still expects to see him at the table. The loss sits beside the hope without either one giving way, and that is what makes the letter hard to read.

By the third winter the letters are longer and quieter. He writes about the light on the water at dawn, about a gull that follows the boat, about the promise he made to his mother to come back by spring. Hope after loss is not a cure in these pages. It is a habit he keeps, the way he keeps writing on Sundays whether or not the post goes out.
