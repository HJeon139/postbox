# Pond journal

A year of short entries kept beside a small pond at the edge of the woods. The writer visits most mornings and writes down what has changed: the ice, the birds, the level of the water after rain.

In winter the pond is a sheet of grey ice and the woods are silent. The entries are short, a few words about the cold and the tracks of a fox across the snow. There is a kind of patience in them, as if the writer has decided that waiting is also a way of paying attention.

Spring arrives all at once. The ice breaks in a single night, the frogs start up, and the trees along the bank turn green from the bottom branches up. The writer notes the first heron, standing in the shallows for most of an hour, and the first warm morning when the whole pond seems to breathe.

Summer is crowded and loud. Dragonflies, swimmers, a family of ducks that crosses the water at the same time every evening. The journal fills with lists. By autumn the lists get shorter again, and the last entries are about the leaves on the water and the light going early, and a memory of the winter before, which no longer seems so long.
