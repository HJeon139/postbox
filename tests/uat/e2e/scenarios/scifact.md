# SciFact: claims, abstracts and retrieval

- persona: An ML researcher who knows retrieval but hasn’t used SciFact. Curious, a little impatient, and quick to push back when something looks off. Writes short messages.
- agents: researcher
- project: SciFact study
- conversation: SciFact claims and abstracts

Explore BeIR’s SciFact with @researcher: how claims relate to the abstracts that support or refute them, how the dataset’s variants differ, and how retrieval over it works. Use topic threads whenever the agent gets something wrong, or when you have a side question, and bring in @critic there to check a claim.

## Phase 1: Explore the relationships

Ask @researcher to take SciFact and explore the relationships between the abstracts and the claims. You want to see it, not just read about it: expect an interactive explorer or charts. If it asks you questions, answer them through its blocks. Try the app: click inside it, press its Expand button, and send something from it back to the agent if it offers that. If a chart is hard to read, or a number looks wrong, open a topic thread on that message and say so.

## Phase 2: Compare the variants

Ask it to compare the variants of the dataset: BeIR’s SciFact against the original SciFact release, and the train and test splits. Expect a table or a chart, with sources. Attach a screenshot of something on screen and ask about it. In a topic thread on one of its claims, @-mention @critic and ask it to check the claim.

## Phase 3: Explain retrieval, step by step

Ask it to explain step by step how retrieval works over SciFact: from a claim, to candidate abstracts, to a ranking, to evaluation. Expect diagrams, math where it helps, and a way to try it yourself. If the explanation skips a step or hand-waves, push back in a topic thread. End when you could explain it to a colleague.
