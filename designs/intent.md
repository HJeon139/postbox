# Design intent

This document records why Postbox looks and behaves the way it does. It’s the reasoning behind its design, worked out in design reviews and the Stage 1 mockups before any app code was written. When a later change conflicts with something here, update this document in the same change, so the reasoning stays current.

## Purpose

Postbox’s value is one loop: you configure an agile team of agents to your vision, walk away while the team builds and holds each other to the definition of done, and come back to a working implementation of what you asked for (the owner’s words, 21 November 2026, [#1604](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1604)). One request goes in. The team takes it as an agile team would: one agent plans, several build, one reviews and one tests, often at once and on the same change, across harnesses from different vendors. What comes back is working software with its evidence attached: the review record, the test results and the version history, recorded from what the agents did. Postbox’s own sprints ran this way, in rooms of 4 to 12 agents (rooms-at-scale.md, “Why”), and the limit they hit was the person’s attention, not building: in one day the person handled about 258 approvals and confirms, about 165 of them a kind they had already allowed ([#852](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/852)). So what Postbox optimizes is the person’s supervision per accepted change, with each time the person had to step in counted inside it. Everything in Postbox serves that loop. Anything that isn’t part of it works against Postbox, because it distracts the agents or degrades what they can do: a card that stops a team while you’re away, a refusal that makes an agent relay data by hand, a check that guards against a risk the agent’s own harness already carries.

Postbox is one place to direct several AI agents at once. You message them, wake them when needed, and each can reply with software it writes on the spot: explainers, data explorers, review surfaces. That software runs live in the conversation, and it’s unloaded when you no longer need it.

Four situations shaped the design:

- **Exploring your own data.** Your notes, papers or records are the conversation’s assets, put there by you or by the agents that brought them in, but you can’t yet say which sources and passages you want. An agent writes an asset explorer mid-conversation. It runs inline, searches the conversation’s assets through Postbox, and sends back the passages you pick.
- **Learning a hard topic.** You want to understand how agents reason and act. An agent pulls papers from arXiv and builds an explainer with citations and a picture you can change to see the idea move, and practice when you ask for it.
- **Watching your own systems.** You run a Ray cluster, and an agent writes an app that shows a job’s live state and progress from the cluster’s dashboard, and its log from disk. The app reads them itself, as often as it needs, so the agent builds the next thing instead of carrying numbers into the app. A team’s live sprint or CI board is the same case: in the 21–22 November 2026 room, a scrum-master’s board app went through seven versions in one day, because its agent rewrote the data and called `update_app` at every standup (the room’s review on [#1605](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/pull/1605)). Planned (#1610): today an app reads only public URLs and the files attached to its version.
- **Giving feedback.** Agents need more than multiple choice. They also need free-form answers, and they need to show you a design, a document or code that you can comment on line by line.

None of these interfaces are built into Postbox. Agents generate them, and Postbox runs them with what they need: the conversation’s assets, public URLs and attached files today, and the person’s own network and files on disk as planned (#1610).

At its core, Postbox’s conversations are a way to manage what each agent remembers. A conversation holds one session per agent, and everything in it, topic and side threads included, is that one session’s work: an agent never has a second copy of itself in a conversation (decided 23 October 2026, #228). To keep a side question out of an agent’s memory, start a new conversation; Compact and Delete are the other controls.

## Function first

Function comes first, and security serves it (decided 21 November 2026, [#1604](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1604)). Security matters, but it doesn’t take a front seat to what agents and their apps can do:
- **A boundary earns its place by what it keeps out.** Each one says what it protects against (designs/architecture/security-boundaries.md). A measure that costs no function stays: the page’s token, the request gate, agent content as text, the sandboxed frame. One that costs function needs a real risk, seen or measured, that the agent’s own harness doesn’t already carry.
- **An app reads live data under a grant its agent sets** (planned, #1610). An agent that can read your files and reach your network shouldn’t have to relay that data into its app turn after turn; that makes it a data transport, spending turns and context. So when an agent shows an app, it names the app’s sources, the endpoints and the folders, inside its working folder or outside it, such as Ray’s log folder, and the app reads them through Postbox’s server with no click from you. The grant is bounded by the agent’s own tools (no Web, no network; no Shell or Read, no files), scoped to that app and its versions, and runs on the agent’s machine. An app isn’t its agent, though: it runs with no turn and no harness judging each read. So the grant has edges that cost a dashboard nothing: credential locations, cloud metadata addresses and other Postbox servers stay out of reach (designs/architecture/security-boundaries.md, “What leaves through net.fetch” and “What an app reads from disk”), and Postbox shows every source the app read.
- **Evidence instead of approvals.** You trust the team’s work by what comes back with it, the review record, the test results, the version history and what each agent ran, not by approving each step while it runs.
- **Walking away is the promise, and this is its acceptance criterion** (no test checks it yet; the UAT to build, [#1613](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1613), is a team that takes one request to a reviewed change, and passes when the person steps in only for a decision on the list below, counting each time they do, with queue time and rework tracked apart, so a fast merge can’t hide how much supervision it took): the team hands off, reviews and reports done without interrupting you. It interrupts only for what only you can decide: merging to `main`, a product or architecture trade-off, spending money, entering a credential, and a destructive step. A confirm before something destructive or costly stays, since those carry decisions only you can make. What still broke it in the 21–22 November 2026 room is loop work, each with its issue: agents replying back and forth after the work was done ([#1553](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1553), [#1591](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1591)); processes another session left running that no agent could attribute ([#1606](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1606)); messages that waited 26 to 167 minutes behind an agent’s own background jobs ([#1614](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1614)), when a background job is there to free its agent for new messages, so an agent with one running takes them right away (planned, designs/architecture/background-processes.md); one `git stash` shared across worktrees ([#1607](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1607)); and a host’s harness found signed out only when a turn failed there ([#1608](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1608)).
- **No approval that buys little security.** A card, confirm or consent step that asks you to approve what the harness already judges, or what the agent could do anyway, stops the team while you’re away and teaches you to click through. It’s left out (principle 8). Designs for agent integrations keep reaching for one; review declines it.

## Who it’s for

One technical person directing a team of agents on their own projects: one agent for deep focus, several for dispatch. Postbox’s own sprints ran rooms of 4 to 12 agents (rooms-at-scale.md, “Why”), and the 21–22 November 2026 room ran eight, across Claude Code, Codex and Kiro, for about 20 hours (the room’s review on [#1605](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/pull/1605), recorded in [#1606](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1606)). They switch between two modes:

- **Dispatch:** you send one request and step away. Several agents plan, build, review and test it in the background, handing work to each other, on this computer and on hosts, and you come back to working software and its evidence. You answer only when one needs a decision only you can make.
- **Deep focus:** a long session with one agent, working through its apps.

Density and speed matter more than onboarding. There is no sign-in and there are no other people in rooms.

Postbox knows who you are ([#558](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/558)): on your own computer, the account it runs as, with nothing to set up. Your initials are in the top bar, and they open Settings on your profile, where you can say who you are in your own words: a name, the other names that are yours elsewhere, pronouns, a time zone and preferences. Two rules keep one person one thing:
- **Agents are told only what you saved.** Your account’s name is kept and shown to you, and is in no turn until you save it, so with nothing saved a turn is what it always was (principle 7: nothing enters an agent’s context that you didn’t write or send). The tab shows exactly what agents are told.
- **Your messages still come from “The person”.** A sender is named only where there is more than one person to tell apart.

It is one system for a run on your own computer and for one hosted for a team: who is asking is answered in one place, and nothing after it asks which kind of run this is ([architecture/people.md](architecture/people.md)). The hosted run isn’t built.

## Principles

1. **Agents author the software; Postbox owns the frame.** Feedback that must be consistent and accessible (choices, forms, confirms, reviews) comes from a fixed catalog that Postbox renders with its own code. Everything else an agent writes runs in a sandbox, behind a bridge that gives it what it needs: the conversation’s assets and public URLs today, and, planned (#1610), the live sources its agent grants it on the person’s own network and disk.
2. **Get the loop right before the looks.** Streaming that never loses a message, statuses that are correct, notifications that fire. If “needs you” can’t be trusted, nothing else matters.
3. **Calm by default; draw the eye only when it matters.** Only blocking events pulse and notify. Every animation eases in, eases out, then rests.
4. **Make good UI the easy path.** Agents do what is easiest, so the kit’s defaults (chart marks, tokens, controls) matter more than any written guidance.
5. **Never rely on color, hover or motion alone.** Every value is also available as text, a label or a table view, and reduced motion is respected everywhere. Every action is visible without hovering; hover only opens previews, such as glossary and citation cards.
6. **You stay in charge of your agents.** New personas need your approval and wake chains are capped. What an agent may run is decided by its harness in the mode you set, and Postbox adds no gate of its own on top (principle 8).
7. **You decide what an agent remembers.** Each agent has one session per conversation, and you choose its memory by where you talk to it: a new conversation starts clean, Compact shortens it, and deleting the conversation ends it. Agents never fork themselves, and nothing from a thread is put in another agent’s context unless that agent chooses to read it or you send it (decided 23 October 2026, #228).
8. **Agents work together without waiting on you; Postbox shows what they did.** Postbox is function first, permissive and collaborative. Agents hand work to each other, on this computer, on hosts and across a feature branch, as fast as their harnesses allow, and Postbox makes their work easy to see rather than asking you to approve it. A card, a confirm, a warning or a notice that waits on you, or asks for your attention, earns its place only when it carries a decision only you can make. One that repeats what you already allowed, that the harness already judges, or that guards against a risk no one has seen, is tedium, not safety, and it’s left out. An alarm you can’t act on is the same tedium: a refused or failed tool call the agent works around, or a change an agent made, never shows as “needs you” or waits for an acknowledgment; it shows as what happened (10 November 2026). When in doubt, show the action where you can see it, and don’t ask (decided 8 November 2026, after [#852](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/852): in one day the person approved about 258 cards and confirms, about 165 of them a kind they had already allowed).

## Where it’s going

[`rooms-at-scale.md`](rooms-at-scale.md) is the design of record for how a room works with four or more agents (direction set by the person on 10 November 2026): each agent’s live state always in view, a thread per piece of work, one stored inbox per agent under mention, wake and queue, asks as the “needs you” interface that several agents can write together, blocks that show state and don’t change on a click, one place and one scope for a persona’s settings, a plain delete, and no permission layer of Postbox’s own. The rows below describe Postbox as built; each one changes in the pull request that builds its part.

[`room-context.md`](room-context.md) is a draft beside it (direction set by the person on 13 November 2026, the rest under review): Postbox as what feeds one conversation to several harnesses, with an @-mention as the only way one agent messages and wakes another, and a conversation an agent can search.

## How the app is organized

```mermaid
flowchart TD
  TB["Top bar: the Postbox menu (projects), the path, the room’s Manage button"] --> P
  P[Project with a root folder] --> T[Direct message: one agent]
  P --> R[Room: several agents]
  T --> S[Session: one per persona per conversation]
  R --> S
  RL["Rail: the open conversation’s agents, then direct messages, rooms and Threads, with rings"] --> T
  RL --> QP["An agent’s name opens its persona sheet"]
  RL --> R
  T --> TT["Topic thread, from any message: the same agents’ sessions, replying there"]
  R --> TT
  T --> SP["Right pane: the conversation’s side views, a thread and its Work, split when both are open"]
  TT --> SP
  T --> A["Inline agent apps and catalog blocks"]
```

Postbox calls the main pane’s threads of messages **conversations**: a direct message with one agent, or a room with several. A **thread** is what starts from a message, as in Slack.

## The loop

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
  participant U as You
  participant A as Postbox
  participant M as MCP server (in Postbox)
  participant G as Agent (any backend)
  U->>A: types a message, or @-mentions an agent
  A->>G: message (wakes the agent if it was idle)
  G-->>A: streamed text
  G->>M: show_block / show_app / define_term
  M->>M: validate against the catalog
  M-->>G: returns at once (never blocks)
  M-->>A: block or app
  A-->>U: renders it (fallback text if invalid)
  U->>A: acts in a block or app
  A->>G: new message carrying { blockId, revision, action, values }
  G->>M: reply, or update the same block id
```

| Decision | Why |

### Writing

Plain English and sentence case, with curly quotes and American spelling everywhere: the UI, the docs and code comments. Buttons say what they do (“Send”, “Stop”, “Open conversation”). Error messages say what happened and what to do next.

### Scope and order

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| The first flow to polish is the asset explorer: an agent writes an app, it runs inline, searches the conversation’s assets through the bridge, and sends picks back | It proves the core idea end to end. Renamed from the corpus explorer on 10 November 2026 (#516), when the corpus went. |
| Postbox keeps its data in a log of events, with a snapshot: a turn’s records are written as one line and synced, so they commit together or not at all. It needs no download and no extra program, and runs on every platform Node does | Decided 31 October 2026 ([#508](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/508)), recalling the 28 October decision for a bundled Redis 8: the slow room is fixed, and a second writer has no user yet. |
| Data agents bring in is a room asset, one versioned store every agent in the conversation reads and writes, here or on a host. There’s no separate corpus: one search tool, `search_assets` (and `assets.search` for apps), runs real BM25 in plain Node over the room’s text assets, with a stub semantic search that your own index replaces later. The demo seeds a few short notes as its explorer’s assets | Decided 10 November 2026 (#516), replacing the bundled public-domain books (5.4 MB) and `add_dataset` (#47, 21 and 23 October 2026). There were two stores for agents’ data and five ways to bring files in, and a dataset couldn’t reach an agent on a host. Assets already are the room’s shared, versioned files, reachable from hosts, so search is just a way to read them. A search covers only its own room, which does what named-only dataset search did: one conversation’s data stays out of another’s results. |
| A demo script plays every block type and every error state | It’s the source of the screenshots once the app is real. |
| An agent’s turns may run on another machine, reached with your own `ssh`. Postbox itself stays on your computer: only the agent’s process moves, and nothing on the host gets a way back to Postbox. Running Postbox itself on the host, served to your browser over a tunnel, isn’t in scope | Decided 23 October 2026 (#120). A remote agent should keep working for days: a turn that’s working never stops because the laptop sleeps or the connection drops, and an idle agent can end its process as long as it resumes easily when you reconnect. Keeping Postbox local keeps it a local app on your own computer, so the page token, the store and `!` commands keep their assumptions (architecture/remote-hosts.md, “Remote hosts”). |

## Not in scope, for now

- Several people, sign-in or presence.
- Using Postbox on a phone. It’s a local web app on your own computer, so effort on phone-only behavior (touch, phone sheets, phone screenshots) is declined. The phone layout row above stays as a note for later. Narrow-width checks at 375 px stay, because a narrow window or an open thread panel squeezes the column the same way. Decided 21 October 2026.
- Search across conversations. Search within a conversation and its side and topic threads is in scope (decided 28 October 2026, [#508](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/508)): the page’s search box since #1155, and an agent’s `search_conversation` since [#1340](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/1340).
- OS notifications and sound.
- Narrated slideshows as a built-in format. Narration arrives in Stage 3 instead, with the real adapters: a speech tool on the host (for example Kokoro) and two recipes, a narrated walkthrough app with a synced transcript and a Slidev export for sharing.
- New catalog components without an explicit decision.

- Task-based testing: timed tasks done in the demo, such as answering a blocked agent from the sidebar, or finding why a reply failed and retrying it.
- A review by someone other than the author.
