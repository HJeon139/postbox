# Compound interest: why it pulls away from simple interest

- **Idea:** Compound interest earns interest on past interest, so it starts almost level with simple interest and then pulls away faster every year.
- **For:** Someone opening their first savings or retirement account.
- **Question:** Why does compound interest end up so far ahead, when the rate is the same?
- **Facts** (arithmetic; the formulas are on [Investor.gov’s compound interest calculator](https://www.investor.gov/financial-tools-calculators/calculators/compound-interest-calculator)):
  - $1,000 at 5% a year. Simple interest adds $50 every year; compound interest adds 5% of the current balance, compounded yearly.
  - After 10 years: $1,500 simple, $1,628.89 compound.
  - After 20 years: $2,000 simple, $2,653.30 compound.
  - After 30 years: $2,500 simple, $4,321.94 compound.
  - Compounded yearly at 5%, the money doubles in about 14.2 years.
- **Shape:** Change over time: two quantities that diverge.
- **Must show:** the two lines nearly together early on and far apart later; that the gap itself grows each year; where the compound balance doubles.
- **Traps:** a table of years; bars for only the final values, which hide that the gap grows; a single “$4,321.94” in a card.
