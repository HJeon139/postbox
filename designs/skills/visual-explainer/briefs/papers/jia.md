# A cell’s clock costs energy: oscillation strength and its limits

- **Idea:** Pushing a molecular cycle one way makes its rhythm stronger but costs more energy, and a simple four-state cycle can never get past about 17%.
- **For:** Someone new to the field, with undergraduate chemistry.
- **Question:** How strong can a random oscillation in a cell be, and what does it cost?
- **Facts** (Jia, [arXiv:2610.23310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23310), preprint, submitted October 16, 2026):
  - A molecule cycles through states, and a bias makes it go one way round more often. That makes its rhythm clearer: the power spectrum grows a peak away from zero frequency.
  - Oscillation efficiency η compares that peak with the zero-frequency background: η = 1 − G(0)/max G (Eq. 1).
  - A clearer rhythm costs energy: entropy production rises with the bias, and the paper gives a lower bound on it (Eq. 27).
  - Any four-state network is capped at cos²(π/4) = 50% efficiency (Eq. 13). The simplest four-state ring stops near 17.2% even when it runs one way only (Eq. 14).
- **Shape:** Trade-off across one setting: the bias.
- **Must show:** one setting (the bias) and what it changes; the efficiency rising toward a ceiling it can’t pass; the energy cost rising with it; the 17.2% ring ceiling under the 50% universal one.
- **Traps:** four numbers in cards as the main view (the room’s first versions); a decorative curve standing in for the computed one; stating the ceiling right above a question that asks for it.
