# Home budget: where this year’s extra spending went

- **Idea:** The total rose only $1,521, because last year’s one-off $1,240 vet bill hides it: the costs that recur each month rose about $2,700, mostly rent after the move, then dining out and subscriptions, with transport down.
- **For:** Someone who keeps a spending sheet and feels this year cost more without knowing where it went.
- **Question:** My spending went up this year; where did it go?
- **Facts** (from the run’s `spending.csv`, 24 months, November 2024 to October 2026; this year is the last 12 months):
  - Total: $35,790 the year before, $37,311 this year, up $1,521.
  - Rent: $1,650 a month until April 2025, then $1,980 from May: $21,780 → $23,760, up $1,980.
  - Dining out: about $175 a month before the move, about $330 after: $3,028 → $3,983, up $955.
  - Subscriptions: $42 a month rising in steps to $96: $713 → $1,092, up $379.
  - Transport: about $215 a month before the move, about $85 after: $1,772 → $1,066, down $706.
  - Other: $1,891 → $694, down $1,197, because August 2025 held a one-off $1,240.
  - Groceries (+$92) and utilities (+$18) barely moved; utilities are seasonal, high every winter.
- **Shape:** Comparison: the change in each category between two years, which adds up to the total.
- **Must show:** each category’s change, up or down, adding up to the total; that the one-off is what hides the rise; that the move is when rent, dining out and transport changed.
- **Traps:** a pie chart of one year; a line per category over 24 months with seven tangled lines; the total alone; utilities’ winter peaks read as growth.
- **Data, not a concept:** the right form here is a chart. An illustration in its place is decoration (VE10).
