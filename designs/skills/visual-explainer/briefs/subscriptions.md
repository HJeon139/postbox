# Subscriptions: which ones are worth it

- **Idea:** What a subscription costs matters less than what it costs per use. Six of the 18 cost $9 or more each time they’re used, or are never used, and they make up $170.92 of the $283.28 a month. Eight cost under $1 a use.
- **For:** Someone who pays for a lot of subscriptions and suspects some aren’t worth it.
- **Question:** Which of my subscriptions are worth it?
- **Facts** (from the run’s `subscriptions.csv`: 18 services, each with its monthly cost and its uses in the last 90 days):
  - All 18 cost $283.28 a month, $3,399.36 a year.
  - Per use, over the last 90 days: the meditation app ($6.99 a month) was never used. The gym ($59.00) costs about $44 a use, the meal kit ($71.96) about $36, and the second video service ($17.99) about $18. The magazine app ($9.99) and the recipe app ($4.99) cost about $15 a use.
  - Those six together cost $170.92 a month, 60% of the total.
  - The gym, the meal kit and the second video service alone cost $148.95 a month, $1,787.40 a year.
  - Eight services cost under $1 a use, $57.44 a month together: music streaming, cloud photo storage, the password manager, phone backup and others used most days.
- **Shape:** A relationship between two quantities, cost and use, per item: a scatter, with lines of equal cost per use.
- **Must show:** every service placed by what it costs and how often it’s used; where cost per use is high; that the expensive services aren’t all poor value and the cheap ones aren’t all good.
- **Traps:** a bar chart of monthly cost, which ranks by price and hides use; a bar of cost per use, which hides whether a service is expensive or just unused; a table sorted by one column.
