# Vaccine trial: what “95% effective” means

- **Idea:** “95% effective” compares two groups: about 1 in 20 as many vaccinated people got sick as people given a placebo, and the estimate has a range.
- **For:** A reader who saw the headline number and wants to know what it measures.
- **Question:** What does “95% effective” mean in the trial?
- **Facts:**
  - The Pfizer-BioNTech trial enrolled more than 43,000 participants. Efficacy against symptomatic, laboratory-confirmed COVID-19 in people without prior infection was 95.0%, with a 95% confidence interval of 90.3% to 97.6% ([CDC MMWR 69(50), December 2020](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/69/wr/mm6950e2.htm)).
  - In that analysis, 8 cases occurred in the vaccine group and 162 in the placebo group, in groups of about 18,000 each ([Polack et al., NEJM 2020](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2034577)). **Not re-checked:** the journal page refused an automated read on 6 November 2026; check these counts against the paper before using the brief.
  - Efficacy is one minus the ratio of the two groups’ rates: 1 − 8/162 is about 95% when the groups are about the same size.
- **Shape:** Comparison with uncertainty.
- **Must show:** the two groups side by side at the same scale; how few cases each group had out of thousands; the range around 95%.
- **Traps:** a big “95%” in a card; reading it as “95% of vaccinated people are protected”; drawing the case counts without the group sizes.
