# Seasons: why summer and winter happen

- **Idea:** Earth’s tilted axis keeps pointing the same way as Earth orbits, so each hemisphere takes turns leaning toward the Sun and getting its more direct light; distance from the Sun isn’t the cause.
- **For:** A curious adult who half-remembers that it has something to do with the Sun.
- **Question:** Why is it summer in one hemisphere while it’s winter in the other?
- **Facts** ([NASA Space Place, “What causes the seasons?”](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/)):
  - Earth’s axis is tilted, and it points the same direction in space all year.
  - As Earth orbits, the hemisphere tilted toward the Sun gets the Sun’s more direct rays and has summer; the other has winter.
  - Earth is closest to the Sun (about 91.4 million miles) in January and farthest (about 94.5 million miles) in July, so the Northern Hemisphere has winter when Earth is closest.
- **Shape:** Mechanism in space: the cause is a geometric arrangement.
- **Must show:** the axis pointing the same way at opposite sides of the orbit; which hemisphere faces the Sun more directly at each; that the closest point falls in northern winter.
- **Traps:** a stretched oval orbit that suggests distance causes seasons; an axis that changes direction around the orbit; the tilt drawn without the sunlight’s angle on the ground.
