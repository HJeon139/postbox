# The brief

Write it before drawing anything. It is the context a reviewer needs to judge the visual, and the yardstick for the takeaway test. Keep it to what fits on one screen.

## Fields

| Field | What it holds | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Idea** | The one thing the reader should come away with, as a sentence with a verb. | Could a reader disagree with it? A topic (“the water cycle”) can’t be disagreed with; an idea can. |
| **For** | Who reads it and what they already know. | Names a person, not “everyone”. |
| **Question** | The question the visual answers, in the reader’s words. | The idea is its answer. |
| **Facts** | Each fact the idea depends on, with its source: a link, a file, a figure or table in a paper. Mark anything you invented. | Every number and every part in the visual traces to one of these. |
| **Shape** | The content’s shape, from [shapes.md](shapes.md), and in one line why. | Comes from the facts, not the topic. |
| **Must show** | The two or three relations the reader has to see: what connects to what, what is bigger, what comes first. | Each one is something you can point at in the picture. |
| **Traps** | What a careless drawing would get wrong. | Optional, but write it when the field has a common misconception. |

## Example

The library flow from the [#206 field notes, Nov 4](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/206#issuecomment-6644256875):

- **Idea:** Two separate paths, each with its own check, meet at the branch shelf, where the reader’s path begins.
- **For:** The library network’s staff and readers.
- **Question:** Where do new books come from, how are they checked, and how do readers get them?
- **Facts:**
  - New books arrive two ways: publisher orders and donations.
  - Orders are cataloged at the central branch; donations get a condition check at the bindery. Different teams run each.
  - Both end on a branch shelf.
  - A reader reserves a book online and picks it up at a branch.
- **Shape:** A flow: two sources merge at one place, then a separate path leads out.
- **Must show:** the two checks as separate; both paths reaching the shelf; the reader’s path starting there.
- **Traps:** merging the two checks into one “checks” step, though different teams run them.

The first try at this ([the field notes’ round 1](https://github.com/HJeon139/postbox/issues/206#issuecomment-6644256875)) wrote each fact as a sentence in a box. The version accepted then drew each thing as an icon with a two-word name, gave each path its own line, and put the prose in the caption. It was still boxes with labels, so it would fail VE10 today: a drawing of the two routes, the cart from the publisher and the box from a donor reaching the shelf, would carry the same brief.
